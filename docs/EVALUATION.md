**Result.** With KEV as an input, VulnTriage puts 10 of 11 KEV findings in its top 10. The baselines put 0 (CVSS-only) and 0 (EPSS-only) there.

Without the KEV signal, no KEV finding reaches the top 20: the median KEV finding sits at rank 50, compared with 479 for CVSS-only and 89 for EPSS-only. In this dataset, non-KEV findings with higher EPSS on exposed services outrank the KEV CVEs. Exploit-prediction signals alone do not reproduce KEV, which is why KEV is a hard tier rather than just another weight.

Exposure and fix context still help: even without KEV, the median KEV finding ranks 9.6× closer to the top than with CVSS-only and 1.8× closer than with EPSS-only.


**How to read this.** VulnTriage uses KEV membership as an input, so its own KEV recall is high by construction. That is the intended behaviour, but it is not evidence of predictive skill. The **KEV-ablated** row removes the KEV and ransomware terms and ranks on EPSS × exposure (plus the fix bonus and CVSS tiebreak) alone. It shows how well the non-KEV signals surface exploited bugs, which matters for the window before CISA lists a CVE. The CVSS-only baseline is what many scanners sort by. Ties are broken by finding id, so baselines with many equal scores (CVSS 9.8, EPSS 0) are not flattered by a lucky order.

## Reproduce

Demo (no Docker, network or API key; numbers above):

```bash
pip install -e ".[dev]"
vulntriage eval --demo            # rebuilds the demo DB, then writes docs/EVALUATION.md
```

On your homelab, after a few nightly runs (or `vulntriage run` a few times):

```bash
vulntriage eval --output docs/EVALUATION.homelab.md --reports 14
# or inside Docker:
docker compose exec vulntriage vulntriage eval --output /reports/EVALUATION.md
```

To measure a real model's grounding rate, set `LLM_PROVIDER`/`LLM_MODEL` and the API key
in `.env` and let reports accumulate. Each report stores its verified/checked claim counts,
and `eval` aggregates the last N. Keep homelab results out of the public repo
(`reports/` and `data/` are git-ignored).
